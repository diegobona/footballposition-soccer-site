The 2026 FIFA World Cup is approaching, and Brazil once again enter the tournament as one of the favorites. However, compared with previous editions, the biggest talking point is not only the attacking talent of Vinicius Jr, Raphinha, Neymar, or Endrick. The real question is how Carlo Ancelotti will fit these players into a balanced, structured, and tournament-ready tactical system.
For a website like **footballposition.soccer**, the key is not simply “who are Brazil’s biggest stars?” The more important question is:
**What role does each player play within the formation, and how do these positions connect with each other?**

***

## 1\. Base Formation: 4\-2\-3\-1 Is the Most Logical Starting Point

Brazil are likely to start from a **4-2-3-1 formation** under Ancelotti.
This shape gives Brazil several advantages.
It allows Vinicius Jr and Raphinha to play as wide attacking outlets.
It gives Casemiro and Bruno Guimaraes a double-pivot structure in midfield.
It creates space for Neymar, Paqueta, or Endrick to operate as the attacking midfielder.
It also allows Matheus Cunha to play as a mobile striker rather than a fixed target man.
In simple terms, Brazil are not just stacking attackers. They are using a double pivot to give their front line the freedom to take risks.

## 2\. In Possession: 4\-2\-3\-1 Becomes 3\-2\-5

On paper, Brazil may line up in a 4-2-3-1. But in possession, the structure is unlikely to remain a flat back four. A more realistic attacking shape is a **3-2-5**.
The idea is simple:
One full-back pushes forward, while the other stays deeper.
The two center-backs and the deeper full-back form a back three.
Casemiro and Bruno Guimaraes stay as the midfield two.
Vinicius Jr, Raphinha, the attacking midfielder, the striker, and another advanced player form a five-man attacking line.
The main purpose of this structure is to occupy all five attacking lanes:
Left wing: Vinicius Jr
Left half-space: Neymar or Paqueta
Center: Matheus Cunha
Right half-space: Endrick or Raphinha
Right wing: Raphinha or the overlapping full-back
This is a modern attacking structure. The goal is not to send everyone into the box immediately. Instead, Brazil first occupy width, depth, and the half-spaces before looking for the final action.

## 3\. Vinicius Jr: Left Winger or Inside Forward?

On the formation board, Vinicius Jr is listed as a **LW — Left Winger**. But his real role is closer to a hybrid of **Wide Winger** and **Inside Forward**.
His job is not simply to stay near the touchline and cross the ball. His first task is to start wide enough to isolate the opposing right-back. Once he receives the ball in a one-vs-one situation, Brazil’s attack immediately becomes dangerous.
Vinicius usually has three main attacking routes:
He can accelerate down the outside.
He can cut inside into the left side of the penalty area.
He can draw defenders toward him and then play a cut-back pass into the center.
That is why Brazil need a supporting player in the left half-space. Neymar, Paqueta, or Cunha can all move into this zone to combine with Vinicius.
For footballposition.soccer readers, Vinicius should not be understood as a traditional winger. He is Brazil’s most important **1v1 isolation player**.
In other words, Brazil deliberately create situations where he can attack a defender directly.

## 4\. Raphinha: Brazil’s Right\-Sided Inverted Winger

Raphinha’s role on the right is different from Vinicius Jr’s role on the left.
Vinicius is more of an explosive dribbler.
Raphinha is more of a rhythm changer.
His role can be defined as an **Inverted Winger**. He starts on the right side but often moves inside onto his stronger left foot. From there, he can shoot, cross, combine, or play diagonal passes into the penalty area.
Brazil’s right side may work in two different ways.
In one version, Raphinha stays wide while the right-back remains deeper or moves inside.
In another version, Raphinha moves into the right half-space while the right-back overlaps on the outside.
This creates a difficult decision for the opponent.
If the opposition full-back follows Raphinha inside, the wide channel opens.
If the full-back stays wide, Raphinha can receive between the lines.
So Brazil’s left and right wings are not symmetrical. The left side is more explosive. The right side is more about variation, timing, and combination play.

***

## 5\. Neymar: His Best Role May Be False 10

Neymar’s inclusion is one of the biggest storylines around Brazil.
From a positional perspective, his best role is probably no longer as a traditional left winger. Instead, he fits better as a **False 10**.
A False 10 does not simply stay in the attacking midfield zone and wait for the ball. The role is more fluid.
A False 10 can:
Drop from the number 10 position into midfield.
Receive between the opponent’s midfield and defensive lines.
Pull defenders out of shape.
Play passes into the wide players or striker.
If Neymar starts, he can become Brazil’s main creative hub. If he comes off the bench, he can be the player who changes the rhythm of the match after 60 minutes.
This is especially important against opponents who defend deep. In those matches, Brazil need someone who can create in tight spaces around the edge of the box. Neymar remains one of Brazil’s best options for that role.

## 6\. The Double Pivot: Casemiro Protects\, Bruno Guimaraes Progresses

Brazil’s midfield double pivot may decide how far the team can go.

### Casemiro: Anchor Man

Casemiro’s role is that of an **Anchor Man**.
His job is not to constantly run forward. His main responsibility is to protect the space in front of the center-backs.
When Vinicius Jr and Raphinha push high, Brazil become vulnerable to counterattacks. Casemiro’s value lies in stopping the opponent’s first forward pass and slowing down transitions.
He gives the attacking players freedom because he provides security behind them.

### Bruno Guimaraes: Deep-Lying Playmaker

Bruno Guimaraes plays more like a **Deep-Lying Playmaker**.
He is responsible for receiving the ball from the defenders and moving it into the attacking players. If Bruno is pressed out of the game, Brazil may become too dependent on Vinicius Jr’s individual ability. But if Bruno can turn and play forward, Brazil’s attack becomes much more complete.
The division of labor is simple:
Casemiro stops Brazil from being broken through.
Bruno Guimaraes helps Brazil move the ball forward.

***

## 7\. Out of Possession: Brazil Drop into 4\-4\-2 or 4\-4\-1\-1

Ancelotti is usually pragmatic in tournament football. Brazil may have many attacking stars, but without the ball they will need a compact defensive structure.
Out of possession, Brazil are likely to drop from 4-2-3-1 into either a **4-4-2** or **4-4-1-1**.
The striker remains high.
The attacking midfielder pushes up as a second forward.
Vinicius Jr and Raphinha drop into the left and right midfield positions.
Casemiro and Bruno Guimaraes protect the center.
The back four stays compact.
This defensive shape is not about aggressive pressing all the time. It is more about protecting central areas. Brazil can allow the opponent to move the ball wide, but they cannot allow easy passes through the middle.

## 8\. Key Positional Roles

### Goalkeeper: Alisson — Sweeper Keeper

Alisson is not only a shot-stopper. He can also act as a **Sweeper Keeper**, helping Brazil build from the back and playing long passes toward Vinicius Jr or Raphinha when opponents press high.

### Center-Backs: Marquinhos and Gabriel

Marquinhos is closer to a **Ball-Playing Centre-Back**. He helps Brazil progress the ball from the defensive line.
Gabriel is more of a **Stopper Centre-Back**. He brings physicality, aerial strength, and box defending.
Together, they give Brazil a balanced center-back pairing: one strong passer, one strong defender.

### Full-Backs: Asymmetrical Use

Brazil are unlikely to use both full-backs as aggressively as in the old Dani Alves and Marcelo era.
Instead, Ancelotti may prefer an asymmetrical structure.
One full-back pushes forward.
The other stays deeper.
This helps Brazil avoid being exposed in defensive transitions.

### Defensive Midfielder: Casemiro

Casemiro is the central safety net. His role is not glamorous, but it allows Brazil’s attackers to play with more freedom.

### Central Midfielder: Bruno Guimaraes

Bruno is the connector between defense and attack. If he can receive, turn, and pass forward, Brazil will not rely only on individual dribbling from the wings.

### Left Winger: Vinicius Jr

Vinicius Jr is Brazil’s most important wide threat. His value comes from creating one-vs-one situations, forcing the opposition defense to shift, and opening central space for teammates.

### Right Winger: Raphinha

Raphinha is an inverted winger on the right. He can cross, cut inside, shoot, press, and recover into the midfield line when Brazil defend in a 4-4-2.

### Attacking Midfielder: Neymar, Endrick, or Paqueta

This is Brazil’s most flexible attacking zone.
Neymar is the best False 10.
Endrick is more direct and can attack the box like a second striker.
Paqueta offers more midfield connection and technical balance.

### Striker: Matheus Cunha

Matheus Cunha is not a traditional target man. He is more of a **Mobile Striker**.
He can drop deeper, move wide, combine with midfielders, and create space for Vinicius Jr and Raphinha to attack the box.

***

## 9\. Brazil’s Main Strength: Wide Isolation and Positional Freedom

Brazil’s biggest attacking strength is their ability to do two things at the same time.
First, they can isolate Vinicius Jr and Raphinha in wide one-vs-one situations.
Second, they can allow Neymar, Endrick, and Cunha to rotate through central and half-space areas.
This makes Brazil difficult to defend.
If the opponent stays compact, Vinicius Jr and Raphinha can attack from the wings.
If the opponent’s full-backs move wide, Neymar and Cunha can receive centrally.
If the center-backs step out, Endrick or a late-arriving midfielder can attack the space behind them.
Brazil’s attack is not built around one fixed pattern. It is built around creating multiple problems for the opponent.

***

## 10\. Brazil’s Main Weakness: Defensive Transitions and Space Behind the Full\-Backs

Brazil’s biggest concern is not attacking quality. It is defensive transition.
When Vinicius Jr, Raphinha, and the attacking midfielder all push forward, space can appear behind the full-backs. If Brazil lose the ball and fail to counter-press immediately, opponents can attack those wide spaces quickly.
Ancelotti will need to manage this risk carefully.
He may use three solutions:
Casemiro stays in front of the center-backs.
Only one full-back pushes high at a time.
Bruno Guimaraes controls second balls and helps stop counterattacks early.
If Brazil want to win the World Cup, they cannot rely only on attacking talent. They must also stay disciplined during transitions.

***

## 11\. Tactical Focus Against Group Opponents

### Against Morocco: Control Counterattacking Space

Morocco are disciplined, athletic, and dangerous in transition. Brazil’s first match should not be about attacking recklessly. The key will be protecting the center and avoiding cheap turnovers.
Casemiro and Bruno Guimaraes will be crucial.

### Against Haiti: More Creativity Around the Box

Against Haiti, Brazil may face a lower defensive block. This type of match suits Neymar or Paqueta, because Brazil will need short combinations, quick passing, and creativity around the penalty area.

### Against Scotland: Second Balls and Set Pieces

Scotland can be physical and direct. Brazil must defend crosses, second balls, and set pieces properly. Gabriel, Casemiro, and the striker will all need to help protect the box.

***

## 12\. Conclusion: A More Structured Brazil

Brazil still have the traditional attacking talent associated with Brazilian football:
Vinicius Jr’s explosiveness.
Raphinha’s left-footed threat.
Neymar’s creativity.
Endrick’s directness.
Cunha’s movement.
But under Ancelotti, Brazil should not be seen as a team that relies only on individual flair.
This Brazil side may be built around:
A 4-2-3-1 base shape.
A 3-2-5 attacking structure in possession.
A 4-4-2 defensive shape out of possession.
Vinicius Jr as the left-sided one-vs-one weapon.
Raphinha as the right-sided inverted winger.
Neymar as a False 10 between the lines.
Casemiro and Bruno Guimaraes as the stabilizing midfield pair.
The key phrase for this Brazil team is:
**Talent with structure.**
They still have individual brilliance, but they also need positional discipline. If Ancelotti can combine attacking freedom with defensive stability, Brazil will not only be one of the most entertaining teams at the 2026 World Cup — they will also be one of the teams with a real tactical case for winning it.
