The 2026 World Cup is approaching, and England once again enter the tournament with huge expectations. Compared with the Gareth Southgate era, the biggest change is not one individual player, but the arrival of Thomas Tuchel as head coach.
Looking at the squad, Tuchel’s approach is clear: he has not simply selected the biggest names, but has focused more on balance, role clarity, and tactical suitability. England still have Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham, Bukayo Saka, Declan Rice, John Stones, Marc Guehi, Marcus Rashford, Anthony Gordon, Morgan Rogers, Eberechi Eze, Noni Madueke, Ollie Watkins, and Ivan Toney. But the key question is: how do these players fit into a stable tactical structure?
From the perspective of **footballposition.soccer**, analysing England should not only be about “Can Kane score?” or “Where should Bellingham play?” The more important questions are: how does Kane shape England’s attacking structure, how do Bellingham and Rice balance the midfield, how does Saka maintain width on the right, and how do the full-backs influence England’s possession shape?

***

## 1\. Base Formation: England Are Most Likely to Use a 4\-2\-3\-1

Tuchel’s England are most likely to use a **4-2-3-1** as their base formation.
This system allows England to use Kane, Saka, Bellingham, Rice, and a left-sided runner at the same time, while still protecting the back line with a midfield double pivot. A likely structure could look like this:
Goalkeeper: Jordan Pickford
Defenders: Nico O’Reilly, Marc Guehi, John Stones, Reece James
Double pivot: Declan Rice, Jude Bellingham
Attacking midfield line: Marcus Rashford / Anthony Gordon, Morgan Rogers, Bukayo Saka
Striker: Harry Kane

### ![england base 4231](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260611-145904-0a159d2d-england-base-4231.png)

The key idea behind this 4-2-3-1 is a stable central spine with wide attacking threat.
Kane starts as the striker, but he is not only a penalty-box forward.
Saka provides reliable one-vs-one quality, inside movement, and crossing options on the right.
Rashford or Gordon gives England pace and depth on the left.
Rogers operates as the attacking midfielder, receiving between the centre and the half-spaces.
Rice and Bellingham form the midfield pair: one provides the defensive base, while the other carries the ball forward and arrives late in the box.
This is not an extreme possession system. It is a structure built for tournament football: secure the middle, progress through wide areas, and use Kane as both a connector and a finisher.

***

## 2\. In Possession: 4\-2\-3\-1 Can Become 3\-2\-5

On paper, England line up in a 4-2-3-1. But in possession, the shape can become a **3-2-5**.
The basic logic is simple:
One full-back pushes forward, while the other stays deeper.
The two centre-backs and the deeper full-back form a back three.
Rice and Bellingham hold the second line in midfield.
The front five occupy the left wing, left half-space, central striker zone, right half-space, and right wing.

### ![england possession 325](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260611-145934-5f38e558-england-possession-325.png)

This diagram shows England’s five attacking lanes in possession:
Left wing: Marcus Rashford / Anthony Gordon
Left half-space: Morgan Rogers
Centre: Harry Kane
Right half-space: Bukayo Saka
Right wing: Reece James or another overlapping right-sided player
The key value of this structure is that it gives both Saka and the left-sided runner one-vs-one opportunities, while still keeping the midfield protected.
If the opponent defends narrow, England can switch the ball to Saka.
If the opposing right-back pushes high, Rashford or Gordon can attack the space behind.
If Kane drops deep, Bellingham can run beyond him into the box.
If the opponent marks Bellingham tightly, Rogers can receive and turn in the attacking midfield zone.
That is the value of Tuchel’s system: it may not make every attack look spectacular, but every zone has a clear receiving point.

***

## 3\. Kane’s Position: Not Just a Striker\, but a Drop\-and\-Link Forward

Kane is listed as **ST — Striker** on the formation board. But his real role is not that of an ordinary striker.
He is better described as a:
**Drop-and-Link Striker**
or
**Complete Forward**
Kane’s value is not only scoring goals. He can drop into the space between midfield and defence, receive the ball, and release runners on either side.

### ![england kane drop release](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260611-145958-a10f4568-england-kane-drop-release.png)

The key point of this diagram is that when Kane drops, England create multiple forward-running options.
First, Saka can move inside from the right or make a diagonal run.
Second, Rashford or Gordon can attack the space behind from the left.
Third, Bellingham can arrive late from midfield and attack the central channel.
Fourth, Rogers can support behind Kane and become the second creator.
So Kane is not just a target man. He is the connection point in England’s attacking structure.
This is both England’s strength and their risk. If Kane is in good form, England’s attack becomes complete. If he is contained, England can become too reliant on wide progression without enough central connection or finishing presence.

***

## 4\. Bellingham: Number 8\, Number 10\, or Free Midfielder?

Bellingham is one of the hardest England players to define positionally.
He can play as a **CM — Central Midfielder**, and he can also play as an **AM — Attacking Midfielder**. But in Tuchel’s 4-2-3-1, he is more likely to operate somewhere between a number 8 and a number 10.
His role can be understood as:
**Box-to-Box Midfielder**
plus
**Late Runner**
Bellingham’s biggest value is not standing permanently as a fixed attacking midfielder. It is starting from a slightly deeper area, then suddenly arriving in the box. He can help Rice progress the ball, but he can also move beyond Kane when Kane drops.
This creates major problems for opponents.
If a centre-back follows Kane out, Bellingham can attack the space behind.
If a defensive midfielder tracks Bellingham, Kane can receive between the lines.
If the opponent collapses centrally, Saka can get space on the right.
Bellingham is not just an individual star. He is a key rotating piece in England’s attacking structure.

***

## 5\. Rice: Midfield Base and Transition Protection

Rice’s position is **DM — Defensive Midfielder**, but his role is not limited to basic defensive midfield work.
He is closer to an:
**Anchor Man**
and
**Transition Defender**
If Tuchel wants England to push their full-backs and wide players high, someone must hold the centre. That player is Rice.
His tasks include:
Protecting the space in front of the centre-backs.
Slowing down counterattacks immediately after England lose the ball.
Helping the centre-backs deal with second balls.
Acting as a passing outlet in deeper build-up.
Stepping forward when needed for shots or second-phase attacks.
Rice’s value does not always show up through spectacular passing. It shows up in the fact that England are not easily played through.

***

## 6\. Saka: England’s Most Reliable Right\-Side Outlet

Saka is England’s most reliable wide attacking outlet. His position is **RW — Right Winger**, but his role is not just that of a traditional winger.
He is closer to an:
**Inverted Winger**
and
**Wide Playmaker**
Saka can do three things:
First, he can receive wide and combine with the right-back.
Second, he can cut inside onto his left foot to shoot or play through balls.
Third, he can attract defenders and release the ball to Bellingham or an overlapping right-sided player.
Much of England’s right-side stability comes from Saka. He is not a pure speed winger who relies only on sprinting. He can keep the ball under pressure, win fouls, and wait for support.
That matters in tournament football. In knockout matches, where the tempo is often tight and tense, a winger who can retain possession is often more valuable than one who only runs fast.

***

## 7\. The Left\-Side Choice: Rashford or Gordon?

England’s left side is mainly about speed and depth.
If **Marcus Rashford** starts, England’s left side becomes more focused on counterattacks and direct runs. He is closer to a **Left-Sided Transition Forward**. When Kane drops or Saka attracts defenders, Rashford can attack the space behind the opposition right-back.
If **Anthony Gordon** starts, England’s left side becomes more about pressing and repeated running. He is closer to a **High-Intensity Winger**. His strengths are off-ball movement, counter-pressing, and wide defensive work.
These two choices represent different match plans:
Rashford starts: better for counterattacks and attacking depth.
Gordon starts: better for high pressing and defensive discipline.
Eze can also be used from the left or centrally as a creative substitute against deep blocks.
This means England’s left side is not a fixed answer. It is a tactical position that can change depending on the opponent.

***

## 8\. Morgan Rogers: The Connector at Number 10

Morgan Rogers could be one of the most interesting roles in this England team.
He is not a traditional number 10 who only waits centrally for the ball. He is also not a pure winger who stays outside. He is closer to a:
**Hybrid Attacking Midfielder**
or
**Left Half-Space Creator**
His value is connecting Kane, the left winger, and midfield.
When Kane drops, Rogers can push forward.
When the left winger holds width, Rogers can move into the left half-space.
When Bellingham drives forward, Rogers can drop and help midfield circulation.
This role may not be the most eye-catching, but it fits Tuchel’s need for system players.

***

## 9\. The Back Line: Stones Builds\, Guehi Defends\, Full\-Backs Balance the Shape

England’s defensive line is not just about reputation. The key question is whether it can support Tuchel’s possession structure and defensive transitions.
**John Stones** is a **Ball-Playing Centre-Back**.
He can carry the ball out from defence and move closer to midfield during build-up.
**Marc Guehi** is more of a **Defensive Centre-Back**.
His job is to hold position, deal with duels, and protect the box.
**Reece James** is an **Attacking Full-Back**.
If fit, he can provide overlapping runs, crossing quality, and progression on the right.
**Nico O’Reilly**, or another left-back option, must find the balance between attack and defence.
With Luke Shaw not in the squad, England’s left-back position is less settled than before. That may affect the quality of England’s left-side progression.
England’s defensive stability will depend heavily on how well they protect the space behind the full-backs when they push forward.

***

## 10\. Out of Possession: England Can Drop into 4\-4\-2

Without the ball, England are likely to shift from 4-2-3-1 into a **4-4-2**.
Kane stays highest.
Rogers moves up as the second forward.
Saka and Rashford or Gordon drop into wide midfield roles.
Rice and Bellingham protect the centre.
The back four stay compact.

### ![england defensive 442](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260611-150043-cbd2cb14-england-defensive-442.png)

The purpose of this defensive structure is not constant high pressing. It is central compactness.
Kane cannot be expected to press like a young forward for the whole match, so England need to defend through good team distances. Rogers can help Kane block the first central pass. Saka and the left winger can recover into wide midfield positions. The midfield pair protects the centre.
This 4-4-2 shape suits tournament football because it is clear and role-based. The risk is that if the wingers do not recover quickly, the full-backs can be exposed in two-vs-one situations.

***

## 11\. Key Positional Roles

**Jordan Pickford: Sweeper Keeper**
Pickford is not only a shot-stopper. His long passing can help England find Kane, Saka, or the left-sided runner quickly.
**John Stones: Ball-Playing Centre-Back**
Stones is England’s main build-up defender. He can carry the ball forward and help midfield in possession.
**Marc Guehi: Defensive Centre-Back**
Guehi provides defensive stability, duel strength, and box protection.
**Reece James: Attacking Full-Back**
Reece James can provide overlapping runs and crossing on the right, but the space behind him needs midfield cover.
**Declan Rice: Anchor Man**
Rice is England’s midfield safety net. He protects the space in front of the centre-backs and is crucial in defensive transitions.
**Jude Bellingham: Box-to-Box Midfielder**
Bellingham provides progression, duels, and late box arrivals. He is England’s most dynamic central midfielder.
**Morgan Rogers: Hybrid Attacking Midfielder**
Rogers connects midfield and attack. He can operate as a number 10, in the left half-space, or as a second forward.
**Bukayo Saka: Inverted Winger**
Saka is England’s most reliable right-side outlet. He can retain possession, cut inside, cross, and create chances.
**Marcus Rashford / Anthony Gordon: Left-Sided Runner**
Rashford is better for attacking depth and counterattacks. Gordon is better for pressing intensity and defensive work.
**Harry Kane: Drop-and-Link Striker**
Kane is both a finisher and a connector. When he drops, he releases the runs of Saka, Rashford, Gordon, and Bellingham.

***

## 12\. England’s Biggest Strength: A Strong Central Spine

England’s biggest strength is the quality of their central spine.
Pickford in goal.
Stones and Guehi in defence.
Rice and Bellingham in midfield.
Kane up front.
Saka as the stable right-side outlet.
This central spine gives England strong tournament potential. Even if they do not always play beautiful football, they have enough quality to win through set pieces, transitions, Kane’s finishing, and Saka’s right-side threat.
This may not be the most glamorous England team, but it may be closer to the kind of practical, winning team Tuchel wants.

***

## 13\. England’s Main Concern: Creativity and Kane Dependence

England’s biggest attacking issue is whether they are too dependent on Kane.
If Kane plays well, England have a reference point, a finisher, and a final passer.
If Kane is contained, England need Bellingham, Saka, Rogers, Rashford, Gordon, or Eze to create more.
Another issue is that without Foden and Palmer, England have lost two players who are very strong in tight spaces. That may make it harder for England to break down low defensive blocks.
So Tuchel must solve three questions:
How can England avoid making Kane responsible for everything?
How can Bellingham and Saka receive in their best zones?
How can Rogers, Eze, and Madueke be used effectively in specific match situations?

***

## 14\. Tactical Focus in the Group Stage

England’s group-stage opponents are Croatia, Ghana, and Panama.

### Against Croatia: Control the Midfield Rhythm

Croatia are experienced and technically strong in midfield. England cannot rely only on wide running. Rice and Bellingham must protect the centre and disrupt Croatia’s rhythm.

### Against Ghana: Be Careful With Speed and Transitions

Ghana are athletic and quick. If England’s full-backs push too high, the space behind them could become dangerous. Rice and the centre-backs will be crucial in defensive transition.

### Against Panama: Patience Against a Low Block

Panama may defend deeper. England will need combinations between Saka, Rogers, Eze, Bellingham, and Kane around the edge of the box. They cannot rely only on crosses.

***

## 15\. Conclusion: England’s Keyword Is “Structure With Efficiency”

England in 2026 may not be the most glamorous version of England on paper, but they could be a team that fits Tuchel’s tournament logic.
This team are likely to use:
A 4-2-3-1 base formation.
A 3-2-5 attacking structure in possession.
A 4-4-2 defensive shape out of possession.
Kane as a drop-and-link striker.
Saka as the stable right-side outlet.
Bellingham as the midfield runner and box-arrival threat.
Rice as the transition protector.
Rashford or Gordon as the left-sided depth runner.
Rogers as the connector between midfield and attack.
The best phrase to describe this England team is:
**structure with efficiency.**
The structure is clear, and the attack is built around efficiency.
If Tuchel can reduce England’s overdependence on Kane while allowing Bellingham and Saka to operate in comfortable zones, England have every chance to go far at the 2026 World Cup.
