The 2026 World Cup is approaching, and France remain one of the strongest title contenders. According to Reuters, Didier Deschamps has named his 26-man squad, with Kylian Mbappe, Ousmane Dembele, Michael Olise, Aurelien Tchouameni, N’Golo Kante, William Saliba, Jules Kounde, Theo Hernandez, and Mike Maignan among the key players selected. France are in Group I alongside Senegal, Iraq, and Norway. Deschamps has also warned his players not to look too far ahead, stressing that France must first focus on their group-stage opponents.
For a website like **footballposition.soccer**, the most important question is not simply whether France have a strong squad. The real focus should be: **what position does each player occupy, what role does he perform, and how do these positions connect tactically?**

***

<br>
## **1\. Base Formation: France Are Likely to Use a 4\-2\-3\-1**

France’s most likely base shape under Deschamps is a **4-2-3-1 formation**. This fits his usual tactical logic: keep attacking speed and individual quality in the front line, while maintaining defensive balance behind the ball.
A likely structure would look like this:
Goalkeeper: Mike Maignan
Back four: Theo Hernandez, Ibrahima Konate, William Saliba, Jules Kounde
Double pivot: Aurelien Tchouameni, N’Golo Kante
Attacking midfield line: Kylian Mbappe, Michael Olise, Ousmane Dembele
Striker: Marcus Thuram
The advantages of this system are clear.
France have direct speed on the left through Mbappe and Theo Hernandez.
They have dribbling, creativity, and technical variation on the right through Dembele and Olise.
They have central protection through Tchouameni and Kante.
They have a mobile striker in Thuram, who can occupy center-backs and create space for the wide attackers.

## **2\. In Possession: 4\-2\-3\-1 Can Become 3\-2\-5**

On paper, France may start in a 4-2-3-1. But in possession, the shape can easily become a **3-2-5 attacking structure**.
The logic is simple:
One full-back pushes high, while the other stays deeper.
The two center-backs and the deeper full-back form a back three.
Tchouameni and Kante hold the second line in midfield.
The front five occupy the left wing, left half-space, center, right half-space, and right wing.
In this structure, France’s left side may become the main attacking channel. Theo Hernandez can push high and provide width, while Mbappe moves inside into the left half-space as a **Left Half-Space Forward** or **Left Inside Forward**.
On the right side, France can be more flexible. Dembele can move into the right half-space, while Olise holds width. Alternatively, Dembele can stay wide and Olise can move inside. This means France’s right side is not just about crossing. It is a technical combination zone designed to create tempo changes and weak-side attacks.

## **3\. Mbappe: Not a Traditional Left Winger\, but a Left Inside Forward**

On a formation board, Mbappe is usually listed as **LW — Left Winger**. But his real role is not that of a traditional left winger.
A traditional left winger often stays wide, beats the full-back, and crosses. Mbappe’s most dangerous zone is different. He starts from the left side, then attacks diagonally toward the channel between the opposing right-back and right center-back.
That makes him better described as:
**Left Inside Forward**
or
**Left-Sided Transition Forward**
His value is not simply in holding the touchline. It is in using speed, timing, and acceleration to attack space. Once France create a one-vs-one situation for Mbappe on the left, the opponent’s right-back and right center-back are forced to shift toward him. That opens space for Thuram centrally and for Olise or Dembele on the far side.

## **4\. Theo Hernandez: France’s Outside Runner on the Left**

Theo Hernandez is listed as **LB — Left Back**, but his role is much more attacking than a basic defensive full-back.
When Mbappe moves inside, France need someone to maintain width on the left touchline. That player is usually Theo. He can push forward from left-back, become a temporary wing-back, and even occupy the left-wing zone in possession.
This relationship is crucial.
Mbappe moves inside and attracts the opposing right-back.
Theo overlaps and stretches the defensive line.
Thuram occupies the center-backs.
Olise or Dembele wait on the far side for a switch of play.
This means France’s left side is not just about individual dribbling. It is a complete attacking chain built around wide support, inside movement, and central occupation.

***

<br>
## **5\. Thuram: A Mobile Striker\, Not a Static Target Man**

Marcus Thuram is likely to play as the **ST — Striker**. But he is not a traditional static target man.
He is closer to a **Mobile Striker**.
His tasks include:
Occupying center-backs.
Dropping short to connect play.
Opening inside lanes for Mbappe.
Attacking cut-back passes inside the box.
Leading the first line of pressure without the ball.
This type of striker is very important for France. If the striker only stays in the penalty area, Mbappe has fewer combination options when he cuts inside. But Thuram can drop, move wide, run beyond, and create more variation for France’s front line.

***

<br>
## **6\. The Right\-Side Combination: Dembele and Olise**

France’s right side could be built around the combination of Dembele and Olise.
Dembele can play as a **RW — Right Winger**, but he can also move into the right half-space.
Olise can play as an **AM — Attacking Midfielder**, but he can also operate as an **Inverted Right Winger**.
Both players are comfortable in tight spaces. They can dribble, combine, and create chances from advanced positions. But their profiles are not identical.
Dembele is more explosive and relies more on dribbling and acceleration.
Olise is more controlled, more creative, and better at final passes and tempo management.
This makes France’s right side different from the left. The left side is more about speed and directness. The right side is more about technique, timing, and combination play.
When opponents overload toward Mbappe, France can switch quickly to the right, where Olise or Dembele can receive against a shifting defensive block.

***

<br>
## **7\. Tchouameni and Kante: One Holds\, One Covers**

France’s double pivot is one of the most important parts of the team.
The partnership between Tchouameni and Kante is essentially an **Anchor + Ball-Winning Midfielder** structure.

### **Tchouameni: Anchor Man**

Tchouameni is best understood as an **Anchor Man**.
His job is to stay in front of the center-backs and control the most dangerous central spaces. He does not need to constantly push forward. His priority is to make sure France are not exposed through the middle after losing the ball.
This is especially important when Theo and Mbappe both move high on the left side. If France lose possession, space can appear behind that side. Tchouameni must hold his position and protect the center-backs.

### **Kante: Ball-Winning Midfielder**

Kante is more of a **Ball-Winning Midfielder**.
His strength is not just fixed positioning. It is lateral coverage, recovery runs, defensive transitions, and second-ball pressure.
France’s midfield logic is simple:
Tchouameni holds the center.
Kante covers the spaces that need covering.
This is exactly the kind of midfield structure Deschamps often trusts: it may not be the most possession-dominant setup, but it gives France outstanding defensive resilience.

***

## **8\. Out of Possession: France Drop into a 4\-4\-2 Mid Block**

Without the ball, France are likely to shift from 4-2-3-1 into a **4-4-2 Mid Block**.
The basic defensive shape would be:
Thuram and Olise stay higher as the front two.
Mbappe drops into the left midfield position.
Dembele drops into the right midfield position.
Tchouameni and Kante protect the center.
The back four stay compact.
The purpose of this defensive shape is not constant all-out pressing. It is central compression. France can allow the opponent to circulate the ball in deeper areas. They can also allow the opponent to move the ball wide. What they cannot allow is easy progression through the middle.
This is a classic Deschamps principle: France may not always dominate possession, but their defensive block is difficult to break through.

## **9\. Key Positional Roles**

### **1\. Maignan: Sweeper Keeper**

Maignan is not only a shot-stopper. He can also act as a **Sweeper Keeper**, helping France build from the back. His long passing can also help France find Mbappe or Dembele quickly when opponents press high.

### **2\. Saliba: Ball\-Playing Centre\-Back**

Saliba is closer to a **Ball-Playing Centre-Back**. He can help France progress the ball from the defensive line and remain calm under pressure.

### **3\. Konate: Stopper Centre\-Back**

Konate is more of a **Stopper Centre-Back**. His physicality, speed, and duel strength make him useful against counterattacks and aerial threats.

### **4\. Kounde: Defensive Full\-Back**

Kounde can play as a right-back, but he is not a traditional attacking full-back. He is more of a **Defensive Full-Back**. When Theo pushes high on the left, Kounde can stay deeper and help France form a back three.

### **5\. Theo Hernandez: Attacking Full\-Back**

Theo is a classic **Attacking Full-Back**. His forward runs are a major part of France’s left-sided attacking structure.

### **6\. Tchouameni: Anchor Man**

Tchouameni is France’s central safety net. He protects the space in front of the center-backs and helps prevent counterattacks through the middle.

### **7\. Kante: Ball\-Winning Midfielder**

Kante provides coverage, recovery runs, and ball-winning ability. His presence makes France safer during attacking transitions.

### **8\. Mbappe: Left Inside Forward**

Mbappe is France’s main attacking weapon. He is not just a left winger. He is a left-sided forward who starts wide and attacks inside.

### **9\. Olise: Creative Attacking Midfielder**

Olise can operate as a **Creative Attacking Midfielder**. He brings final passes, tempo control, and weak-side receiving quality.

### **10\. Dembele: Inverted Winger**

Dembele can play as an **Inverted Winger** or a free wide attacker. His dribbling gives France a way to create local breakthroughs.

### **11\. Thuram: Mobile Striker**

Thuram is a **Mobile Striker**. His movement and physical presence create space for Mbappe, Olise, and Dembele.

***

<br>
## **10\. France’s Biggest Strength: Speed\, Depth\, and Balance**

France’s biggest strength is not simply individual quality. It is the way their structure combines speed, power, technique, and defensive discipline.
On the left, Mbappe and Theo can attack space directly.
On the right, Olise and Dembele can create with technique and variation.
In midfield, Tchouameni and Kante protect the defense.
At the back, Saliba, Konate, and Kounde provide speed, strength, and stability.
This means France can play different types of matches.
Against weaker opponents, they can push high and attack in a 3-2-5.
Against stronger opponents, they can defend in a 4-4-2 mid-block and counterattack through Mbappe.
This tactical flexibility is one of the main reasons France are among the tournament favorites.

***

<br>
## **11\. France’s Main Weakness: Can They Create Enough Against Deep Blocks?**

France have an excellent squad, but there is one possible weakness: can they consistently create chances when opponents defend deep?
If Mbappe has no space to run into, France need Olise, Dembele, and the midfielders to create in tight spaces.
If Olise is tightly marked, France may become too dependent on Mbappe’s individual actions.
If Thuram is controlled by opposing center-backs, France may lose an important central reference point.
So the key question is not whether France can counterattack. They can. The bigger question is whether they can create high-quality chances in settled possession.

***

<br>
## **12\. Tactical Focus Against Group Opponents**

France’s group-stage opponents are Senegal, Iraq, and Norway. Deschamps has already warned that France should not think too far ahead and must focus on each group match first. 

### **Against Senegal: Watch the Physicality and Counterattacks**

Senegal are strong, athletic, and dangerous in transition. France cannot afford to lose control of central areas. Tchouameni and Kante will be crucial in protecting the middle and stopping direct attacks behind the defense.

### **Against Iraq: Patience Against a Deep Block**

Iraq may defend deeper. France will need Olise and Dembele to create around the edge of the box. They cannot rely only on crosses from wide areas.

### **Against Norway: Control the Central Striker Threat**

Against Norway, France must pay special attention to the central striker threat and penalty-box duels. Konate, Saliba, and Tchouameni will be especially important in protecting the box.

***

<br>
## **13\. Conclusion: France Are Built on Speed with Structure**

France in 2026 are not just a team with Mbappe. They are a team with a complete tactical framework.
They are likely to use:
A 4-2-3-1 base formation.
A 3-2-5 attacking structure in possession.
A 4-4-2 mid-block out of possession.
Mbappe as a left inside forward.
Theo Hernandez as the left-sided width provider.
Olise and Dembele as the right-sided creative pair.
Tchouameni and Kante as the midfield protection.
Saliba, Konate, and Kounde as the defensive foundation.
The best phrase to describe this France team is:
**speed with structure.**
They are fast, but they are not chaotic. They have elite individual quality, but that quality sits inside a clear tactical framework.
If Deschamps can balance Mbappe’s freedom, right-side creativity, and midfield defensive stability, France will not only be one of the most dangerous counterattacking teams at the 2026 World Cup. They will also be one of the teams with the strongest tactical case for winning it.
