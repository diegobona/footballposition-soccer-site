The 2026 World Cup is approaching, and Portugal remain one of the most talked-about teams in the tournament. On one hand, they have elite players such as Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva, Vitinha, Joao Neves, Nuno Mendes, Ruben Dias, and Rafael Leao. On the other hand, 41-year-old Cristiano Ronaldo is still one of the central figures in the squad.
From the perspective of **footballposition.soccer**, the key is not simply to ask whether Ronaldo can still score goals. The more important question is: how does Ronaldo affect Portugal’s shape, and how do players like Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva, Vitinha, and Rafael Leao operate around him?

***

## 1\. Base Formation: Portugal Are Most Likely to Use a 4\-2\-3\-1

Portugal’s most logical base formation is a **4-2-3-1**.
This system allows Portugal to use Ronaldo, Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva, and Rafael Leao at the same time, while still protecting the defence with a double pivot. A likely structure could look like this:
Goalkeeper: Diogo Costa
Defenders: Nuno Mendes, Ruben Dias, Goncalo Inacio, Diogo Dalot
Double pivot: Joao Neves, Vitinha
Attacking midfield line: Rafael Leao, Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva
Striker: Cristiano Ronaldo

### ![portugal base 4231](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-122330-95bfe1f4-portugal-base-4231.png)

The key idea behind this 4-2-3-1 is balance.
Ronaldo plays as the central striker, focusing on penalty-box finishing and occupying centre-backs.
Leao provides speed and depth on the left.
Bruno Fernandes operates as the attacking midfielder, looking for through balls, long shots, and final passes.
Bernardo Silva plays from the right side or right half-space, helping Portugal with ball retention, rhythm, and short combinations.
Joao Neves and Vitinha form the midfield pair: one provides coverage and pressing, while the other helps with passing and progression.
This is not an old-fashioned system built only around crossing to Ronaldo. More accurately, Portugal will try to use midfield quality and wide speed to deliver the ball into areas where Ronaldo can still be decisive.

***

## 2\. In Possession: 4\-2\-3\-1 Can Become 3\-2\-5

When Portugal have the ball, their 4-2-3-1 can become a **3-2-5**.
The basic logic is simple:
One full-back pushes forward, while the other stays deeper.
The two centre-backs and the deeper full-back form a back three.
Joao Neves and Vitinha hold the second line in midfield.
The front five occupy the left wing, left half-space, central striker zone, right half-space, and right wing.

### ![portugal possession 325](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-122350-51cd2b88-portugal-possession-325.png)

This diagram shows Portugal’s five attacking lanes in possession:
Left wing: Rafael Leao
Left half-space: Bruno Fernandes
Centre: Cristiano Ronaldo
Right half-space: Bernardo Silva
Right wing: Nuno Mendes or Pedro Neto / Francisco Conceicao
The biggest value of this structure is that Portugal do not need to rely on only one attacking route.
If the opponent defends narrow, Leao can receive in space on the left.
If the opponent shifts toward Leao’s side, Bernardo Silva can receive in the right half-space.
If a centre-back steps out, Ronaldo can attack the gap inside the box.
If the opposition defensive midfielder follows Bruno, Vitinha can progress through the middle.
So Portugal’s attack is not simply “cross to Ronaldo from wide areas.” It is about using five attacking lanes to create multiple problems for the opponent.

***

## 3\. Ronaldo’s Position: Traditional Striker or Penalty\-Box Forward?

On the formation board, Ronaldo is listed as **ST — Striker**. But in Portugal’s 2026 team, he is no longer the wide forward who can make long dribbles from the flank, nor is he a false nine who constantly drops into midfield to organize play.
His current role is better described as:
**Box Striker**
or
**Penalty-Box Forward**
His main value is concentrated in three areas:
First, attacking crosses and loose balls inside the box.
Second, occupying centre-backs.
Third, finishing chances in decisive moments.

### ![portugal ronaldo attacking role](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-122418-92de5fad-portugal-ronaldo-attacking-role.png)

The key point of this diagram is that Ronaldo does not need to cover the whole front line. What he really needs is high-quality service.
Leao can beat defenders on the left and deliver crosses or cut-backs.
Bernardo Silva can provide final passes from the right half-space.
Bruno Fernandes can play direct balls behind the defence or clip passes into the box.
Vitinha can progress the ball through the middle and connect with the attacking line.
If Portugal can move the ball consistently into the final third, Ronaldo still has clear tactical value. But if Portugal need their striker to press constantly, drop deep, hold the ball under pressure, and sprint into channels, Ronaldo’s limitations become more visible.
So Ronaldo is not the problem by himself. The real question is whether Portugal can build a smooth supply system around his finishing ability.

***

## 4\. Bruno Fernandes: Portugal’s Vertical Creator

Bruno Fernandes is listed as an **AM — Attacking Midfielder**, but his real role is closer to a **Vertical Creator**.
He is not a slow possession player. He wants to move the ball forward quickly. He often looks for:
Through balls behind the defence.
Diagonal passes to the wings.
Long shots from outside the box.
Quick switches of play.
First-time attacking passes after second balls.
Bruno’s strength is creativity. He can suddenly change the rhythm of a match. His weakness is that his passing risk is higher than that of a pure possession midfielder.
That is why Portugal need Vitinha and Joao Neves behind him. Bruno can take risks, but there must be players behind him who can control the tempo and protect the team in transition.
For Portugal, Bruno is the player who breaks balance. He is not there to make every pass safe. He is there to make Portugal suddenly move forward.

***

## 5\. Bernardo Silva: A Right\-Side Playmaker\, Not a Traditional Winger

Bernardo Silva can be placed as a **RW — Right Winger** on the formation board, but his actual role is not that of a traditional right winger.
He is closer to a:
**Right Half-Space Playmaker**
or
**Wide Playmaker**
He does not simply stay on the touchline waiting to cross. He often moves inside into the right half-space, using ball control, turns, and short combinations to stabilize Portugal’s attack.
Bernardo’s value is that:
He helps Portugal keep the ball.
He attracts opposition midfielders.
He can combine with the right-back, Bruno Fernandes, and Vitinha.
He can also receive weak-side switches and create chances.
If Leao is the explosive threat on the left, and Bruno is the vertical creator, then Bernardo is Portugal’s rhythm controller in the final third.

***

## 6\. Rafael Leao: Wide Winger and Transition Threat on the Left

Leao is Portugal’s most important wide outlet. His position is **LW — Left Winger**, but his value is not just about crossing from the flank.
He is closer to a combination of:
**Wide Winger**
and
**Transition Threat**
Leao is most dangerous when he receives space on the left and attacks the opposition right-back directly. He can beat defenders on the outside or cut inside into the left side of the box.
This creates two problems for the opponent:
If the right-back gets too tight, Leao can use his pace to go outside.
If a centre-back comes across to cover, Ronaldo may get space to attack inside the box.
That is why Leao and Ronaldo have a natural tactical connection. Leao tears the defensive line open. Ronaldo attacks the final space.

***

## 7\. Vitinha and Joao Neves: The Midfield Pair That Defines Portugal’s Ceiling

Portugal’s most important midfield pairing could be **Vitinha + Joao Neves**.

### Vitinha: Deep-Lying Playmaker

Vitinha is best understood as a **Deep-Lying Playmaker**.
He receives from the defenders, carries or passes the ball into midfield, and then connects with Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva, or Leao. His strengths are receiving angles, passing security, and the ability to turn under pressure.
If Vitinha is shut down, Portugal may become too direct, relying too much on wide dribbles and long passes.

### Joao Neves: Ball-Winning Midfielder

Joao Neves is closer to a **Ball-Winning Midfielder** or a **Pressing 8**.
He provides coverage, counter-pressing, defensive pressure, and protection in transition. This is especially important when Bruno and the wide forwards move high. Joao Neves must be ready to cover midfield spaces quickly.
Their division of labor is simple:
Vitinha helps Portugal move the ball forward.
Joao Neves helps Portugal avoid being hit back.

***

## 8\. Nuno Mendes: Portugal’s Second Left\-Side Progressor

Nuno Mendes is listed as **LB — Left Back**, but when Portugal have the ball, his role is much more than basic defending.
He can be used in two ways:
First, he can overlap on the outside to support Leao.
Second, he can push higher and become a temporary wing-back.
If Leao cuts inside, Nuno Mendes can hold the touchline and provide width.
If Leao stays wide, Nuno Mendes can move inside to help midfield progression.
This gives Portugal’s left side multiple layers. It is not only Leao attacking alone. It is a triangle between Leao, Nuno Mendes, and Bruno Fernandes.

***

## 9\. Ruben Dias: Defensive Leader and Rest Defence Anchor

Ruben Dias is the most important player in Portugal’s back line. His role can be described as both a **Defensive Leader** and a **Rest Defence Anchor**.
When Portugal dominate possession, many players push high. That means the back line must be ready to handle counterattacks. Ruben Dias is responsible for holding the defensive line, organizing teammates, and winning duels inside the box.
If Portugal want to go deep in the tournament, they cannot only rely on attacking talent. Defensive stability will be just as important.

***

## 10\. Out of Possession: Portugal Can Drop into 4\-4\-2

Without the ball, Portugal are likely to shift from 4-2-3-1 into a **4-4-2**.
Ronaldo stays highest.
Bruno Fernandes moves up as the second forward.
Leao and Bernardo Silva drop into the left and right midfield positions.
Vitinha and Joao Neves protect the centre.
The back four stay compact.

### ![portugal defensive 442](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-122444-25ec9cc9-portugal-defensive-442.png)

The purpose of this defensive structure is not constant high pressing. It is central compactness.
Ronaldo cannot be expected to press like a young forward across the whole pitch, so Portugal need to defend intelligently. Bruno can help him block the first central pass, Bernardo and Leao can recover into wide midfield roles, and the midfield pair can protect the centre.
The advantage of this 4-4-2 is that it is clear and tournament-friendly. The weakness is that if the wingers do not track back quickly enough, the full-backs can be exposed in two-vs-one situations.
So Portugal’s defensive success is not about how much Ronaldo runs. It is about whether the whole team keeps the right distances.

***

## 11\. Key Positional Roles

**Diogo Costa: Sweeper Keeper**
Diogo Costa is not only a shot-stopper. He also participates in build-up, and his long passing can help Portugal find Leao or Ronaldo quickly.
**Ruben Dias: Defensive Leader**
Ruben Dias is the leader of the defensive line. He organizes the back four, wins duels, and protects the space behind Portugal’s high possession shape.
**Goncalo Inacio: Ball-Playing Centre-Back**
Inacio offers left-footed build-up quality and helps Portugal progress smoothly from defence into midfield.
**Nuno Mendes: Attacking Full-Back**
Nuno Mendes is a key left-side progressor. He can overlap, provide width, and combine with Leao.
**Diogo Dalot: Balanced Full-Back**
Dalot can provide a balanced right-back role. If Cancelo starts instead, Portugal’s right side becomes more attacking, but midfield cover becomes more important.
**Vitinha: Deep-Lying Playmaker**
Vitinha connects defence to attack and is central to Portugal’s possession rhythm.
**Joao Neves: Ball-Winning Midfielder**
Joao Neves provides pressing, coverage, and transition defence. He is Portugal’s midfield safety valve.
**Bruno Fernandes: Vertical Creator**
Bruno looks for forward passes, through balls, long shots, and final actions. He is one of Portugal’s main chance creators.
**Bernardo Silva: Right Half-Space Playmaker**
Bernardo controls tempo, supports combinations, and stabilizes Portugal’s right-side attacking structure.
**Rafael Leao: Wide Winger**
Leao gives Portugal direct speed, left-side dribbling, and transition threat.
**Cristiano Ronaldo: Box Striker**
Ronaldo’s core value is penalty-box finishing, attacking crosses, and occupying centre-backs. Portugal need to give him high-quality service, not ask him to cover the entire front line like a young striker.

***

## 12\. Portugal’s Biggest Strength: So Many Different Creators

Portugal’s biggest strength is the variety of their creative players.
Bruno Fernandes can play through balls.
Bernardo Silva can control rhythm.
Vitinha can progress from midfield.
Leao can beat defenders one-vs-one.
Nuno Mendes can add speed from full-back.
Ronaldo can finish moves inside the box.
This allows Portugal to play different types of matches.
Against weaker teams, they can push high and attack in a 3-2-5.
Against stronger teams, they can defend in a 4-4-2 mid-block and counter through Leao and Ronaldo.
Against deep blocks, they can use Bruno, Bernardo, and Vitinha to search for passing lanes around the edge of the box.
Portugal do not lack attacking options. The challenge is turning those options into a stable system.

***

## 13\. Portugal’s Main Concern: Ronaldo’s Role and Pressing Intensity

Portugal’s biggest tactical issue is still Ronaldo’s role.
If Ronaldo starts, Portugal have stronger penalty-box finishing, but their front-line pressing and transition speed may drop.
If Goncalo Ramos or Joao Felix plays centrally, Portugal become more fluid, but they lose Ronaldo’s huge penalty-box presence.
This is not a simple question of whether Ronaldo should play. It is a tactical trade-off.
If Ronaldo starts, Portugal need more wide service and better second-ball protection.
If Ronaldo comes off the bench, Portugal can increase pressing and attacking fluidity earlier in the match.
If Ronaldo enters in decisive moments, he could still be one of the most dangerous finishers late in tournament games.
Martinez’s hardest task is to respect Ronaldo’s historic status while keeping Portugal’s overall style modern.

***

## 14\. Tactical Focus in the Group Stage

Portugal’s group-stage opponents include DR Congo, Uzbekistan, and Colombia.

### Against DR Congo: Be Careful With Physicality and Transitions

DR Congo are athletic and quick. Portugal cannot afford cheap turnovers in midfield. Joao Neves and Ruben Dias will be crucial in defensive transitions.

### Against Uzbekistan: Patience Against a Low Block

Uzbekistan may defend deep. Portugal will need Bruno, Bernardo, and Vitinha to combine around the edge of the box, rather than relying only on wide crosses.

### Against Colombia: Control the Wide Areas and Second Balls

Colombia have strong individual attackers. Portugal must protect the space behind their full-backs. Dalot, Nuno Mendes, and the midfield pair will need to recover quickly.

***

## 15\. Conclusion: Portugal’s Keyword Is “Creativity With Compromise”

Portugal in 2026 have excellent squad depth. They can use a technical midfield, fast wide players, and Ronaldo’s penalty-box finishing.
This team are likely to use:
A 4-2-3-1 base formation.
A 3-2-5 attacking structure in possession.
A 4-4-2 defensive shape out of possession.
Bruno Fernandes as the vertical passer.
Bernardo Silva as the right half-space rhythm controller.
Leao as the left-side space attacker.
Vitinha and Joao Neves as the midfield protection.
Ronaldo as the penalty-box finisher.
The best phrase to describe this Portugal team is:
**creativity with compromise.**
They have huge creative quality, but they must make tactical trade-offs.
If Martinez can balance Ronaldo’s finishing value, front-line pressing intensity, and midfield control, Portugal have every chance to go far at the 2026 World Cup.
