# **Where Do They Play?**

The Number 8 starts in central midfield and moves through several phases of the game. The Number 10 starts closer to the striker, in the space between the opponent’s midfield and defense.
![]() ![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/07/20260720-135255-f2f77a7e-image.png)
*The Number 8 works from central midfield; the Number 10 works higher, between the lines.*

# **What Does a Number 8 Do?**

The Number 8 is the midfield connector. He helps the team progress, compete and stay balanced.
· Receives from defenders or the holding midfielder.
· Carries or passes the ball into the attacking half.
· Supports pressing and ball recovery.
· Covers space when full-backs or other midfielders move forward.
· Arrives late in the penalty area rather than waiting there.
Frank Lampard was a classic attack-minded 8: he began in midfield but repeatedly arrived in scoring positions. Steven Gerrard added long passing, ball-carrying and long-range shooting. Jude Bellingham is a modern example because he can defend, drive forward and attack the box in the same match.

# **What Does a Number 10 Do?**

The Number 10 is the attacking creator. His best work usually happens near the striker and around the edge of the penalty area.
· Receives between the lines.
· Turns toward goal and attacks the defensive line.
· Plays through balls and final passes.
· Combines with the striker and wingers.
· Creates or takes shots in the final third.
Zinedine Zidane was a classic Number 10, controlling attacks from an advanced central position. Mesut Özil specialised in finding space and delivering the final pass. Kevin De Bruyne is a more modern case: he creates like a 10 but also covers ground and delivers from deeper positions.

# **How Their Movement Is Different**

The Number 8 moves through the midfield to support several parts of the team. The Number 10 moves around the attacking zone to receive, combine and create.

# **Can a Player Be Both?**

Yes. These numbers describe roles, not permanent identities. A midfielder can begin as an 8 and move into the 10 zone when his team attacks.
Bellingham often does this. He may help build play from midfield, then arrive beside the striker. De Bruyne also sits between the two labels: his final passing looks like a Number 10, while his running range and deeper delivery can resemble a Number 8.
The best test is simple: look at the player’s main responsibility. If he connects several phases of play, he is functioning more like an 8. If he mainly receives high and creates the final action, he is functioning more like a 10.

# **The Simple Difference**

The Number 8 helps the team reach the final third. The Number 10 tries to decide what happens once the team gets there.
Many strong teams use both: the 8 supplies balance, running and connection; the 10 supplies ideas, final passes and attacking quality.
