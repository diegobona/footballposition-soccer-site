The 2026 World Cup is approaching, and Spain remain one of the most tactically interesting teams in the tournament. Unlike the older image of Spain as a team built only around short passing and possession, this new Spain combines traditional positional play with more direct wide attacks.
From the perspective of **footballposition.soccer**, the key question is not simply “Can Spain keep the ball?” The better question is: **how do Spain’s player positions connect, and how does each role help the team create space?**
This article is based on the following likely main structure:
Goalkeeper and defence: Unai Simon; Cucurella, Cubarsi, Laporte, Porro.
Midfield three: Rodri as the deepest midfielder, Pedri as the main creator, and Fabian Ruiz or Gavi as the third midfielder.
Front three: Nico Williams and Lamine Yamal on the wings, with Oyarzabal or Olmo playing centrally.

***

## 1\. Base Formation: 4\-3\-3 Is Spain’s Core Structure

Spain’s most logical base formation is a **4-3-3**.
This shape fits Spain’s football identity very well: build from the back, control midfield, and stretch the pitch with wide players. But this Spain side is not just about midfield passing. With Nico Williams and Lamine Yamal, Spain now have two genuine wide players who can beat defenders one-vs-one.
In the base shape, Spain can line up like this:
Goalkeeper: Unai Simon
Defenders: Cucurella, Cubarsi, Laporte, Porro
Midfielders: Rodri, Pedri, Fabian Ruiz / Gavi
Forwards: Nico Williams, Oyarzabal / Olmo, Lamine Yamal

### ![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-115604-ffc73a1e-image.png)

The key point of this 4-3-3 is not that every player stays fixed in one position. The value is that every line has a clear job.
Rodri is the base of midfield. He controls the tempo and protects the defence.
Pedri connects midfield and attack.
Fabian Ruiz or Gavi gives Spain either more control or more pressing intensity.
Nico Williams and Lamine Yamal stretch the pitch.
Oyarzabal or Olmo gives Spain two different options through the middle.
If Oyarzabal starts, Spain have a clearer central striker who can occupy defenders, attack crosses, and finish moves.
If Olmo starts centrally, Spain become more fluid, because he can act as a **False 9** or an **attacking midfielder used as a striker**.

***

## 2\. In Possession: 4\-3\-3 Becomes 3\-2\-5

On paper, Spain start in a 4-3-3. But in possession, they can shift into a **3-2-5**.
The logic is simple:
One full-back pushes forward, while the other stays slightly deeper.
Cubarsi, Laporte, and the deeper full-back form the back three.
Rodri and another midfielder form the second line.
The front five occupy the left wing, left half-space, central striker zone, right half-space, and right wing.

### ![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-115647-c4bbed13-image.png)

The most important part of this diagram is not the number “3-2-5” itself. The key is how Spain occupy the five attacking lanes.
Left wing: Nico Williams
Left half-space: Pedri
Centre: Oyarzabal / Olmo
Right half-space: Lamine Yamal
Right wing: Porro or Yamal holding width
This structure helps Spain avoid one of the common problems of traditional possession football: having a lot of the ball but lacking width and penetration.
Older Spain teams could sometimes become too narrow, with too many midfielders coming toward the ball. This version is different. Nico and Yamal can stay wide, forcing the opposition back line to stretch horizontally. Pedri and Olmo can receive between the lines. Rodri controls the rhythm from behind.
This is a more modern Spain: **still possession-based, but less slow; still technical, but more vertical.**

***

## 3\. Rodri: The Real Base of the Team

Rodri’s position is **DM — Defensive Midfielder**, but his real role goes far beyond that label.
He combines three roles:
**Anchor Man**
**Deep-Lying Playmaker**
**Rest Defence Controller**
When Spain have the ball, Rodri decides the rhythm of the team. He chooses when to recycle possession, when to play forward, when to switch play, and when to slow the game down.
More importantly, when Spain lose the ball, Rodri must already be in the right place. He does not simply chase the ball. He protects the centre and stops the opponent’s first forward pass from breaking Spain open.
That is Rodri’s real value. He is both the brain of Spain’s possession game and the safety net in defensive transition.

***

## 4\. Pedri: Spain’s Left Half\-Space Playmaker

Pedri can be listed as **LCM — Left Central Midfielder**, but his actual influence is usually higher up the pitch, especially in the left half-space.
He is not a traditional wide midfielder, and he is not only a deep passer. His value comes from:
Receiving between midfield and attack.
Combining with Nico Williams and the central forward.
Attracting pressure and then releasing the ball.
Finding passing angles between opposition lines.
In positional terms, Pedri is best described as an **Advanced 8** or a **Left Half-Space Playmaker**.
This is crucial for Spain’s left side. If Nico Williams is isolated on the left without support, he can be double-teamed. But with Pedri inside him, Spain create a strong left-side triangle:
Cucurella supports from behind or outside.
Pedri receives in the left half-space.
Nico attacks the full-back one-vs-one.

***

## 5\. Fabian Ruiz or Gavi: Two Different Versions of the Third Midfielder

The third midfield spot can go to Fabian Ruiz or Gavi. This choice changes Spain’s style.
If **Fabian Ruiz** starts, Spain get more control. He offers left-footed passing, tempo management, late runs, and shooting from distance. He can help Rodri with build-up and also provide threat around the edge of the box.
If **Gavi** starts, Spain become more aggressive. His strengths are pressing, duels, counter-pressing, and off-ball intensity. He may not control the rhythm as smoothly as Fabian, but he gives Spain more energy when they lose the ball.
So this role gives Spain two different modes:
Fabian Ruiz: more control, smoother possession.
Gavi: more pressing, more intensity.
Against stronger opponents, Gavi’s defensive work may be more valuable. Against deep blocks, Fabian’s passing and shooting may be more useful.

***

## 6\. Nico Williams and Lamine Yamal: Two Wingers\, Two Different Roles

Spain’s front line is exciting because of the two wide players.
Nico Williams on the left.
Lamine Yamal on the right.
Both are wingers, but they do not play the same way.
Nico is more of a **Wide Winger**. His strengths are pace, acceleration, and outside runs. He can pin the opposition right-back deep and carry the ball quickly in transition.
Yamal is more of an **Inverted Winger** or a **Creative Wide Forward**. He starts on the right but often comes inside onto his left foot to pass, shoot, or play through balls.

### ![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-115739-e7db68a5-image.png)

This diagram shows Spain’s wide attacking logic.
On the left, Nico provides width and one-vs-one threat.
On the right, Yamal cuts inside and creates.
In the middle, Oyarzabal or Olmo connects play and occupies defenders.
Pedri acts as the central connector between both sides.
This is why Spain are harder to defend than before. In the past, opponents could reduce Spain’s threat by blocking central areas. Now, if opponents stay narrow, Nico and Yamal get space out wide. If opponents stretch wide, Pedri, Olmo, and Rodri can find central passing lanes.

***

## 7\. Oyarzabal or Olmo: Two Ways to Play the Central Role

The central forward position can be played by Oyarzabal or Olmo. This is a key decision because it changes Spain’s attacking structure.

### Oyarzabal: Mobile Striker

If Oyarzabal plays centrally, he is best understood as a **Mobile Striker**.
He is not a traditional big target man, but he can occupy centre-backs, link play, make runs, and finish chances. He gives Spain a clearer penalty-box presence and can attack crosses or cut-backs from Nico and Yamal.
This setup is better when Spain need a more reliable finishing point.

### Olmo: False 9 / Free 10

If Olmo plays centrally, he is closer to a **False 9** or a **Free 10**.
He can drop from the striker position into the space between midfield and defence. This creates a problem for opposing centre-backs:
If they follow him, space opens behind them.
If they stay back, Olmo can turn and create.
This makes Spain’s central attack more fluid, but it can also reduce their penalty-box presence.
So Oyarzabal gives Spain a clearer striker function. Olmo gives Spain more positional rotation and creativity.

***

## 8\. The Back Line: Cubarsi Builds\, Laporte Stabilizes

Spain’s defence is also tactically important.
Cubarsi fits the modern Spain system very well. He can be described as a **Ball-Playing Centre-Back**. His job is not just to defend, but to help Spain build attacks from the back.
Laporte is more of an **Experienced Centre-Back**. His positioning, left-footed passing, and experience are important for balancing a relatively young defensive unit.
Cucurella is an **Aggressive Full-Back**. He can press forward, support Nico, move inside, and recover quickly when Spain lose the ball.
Porro is an **Attacking Full-Back**. He can provide width on the right, overlap, or move inside in certain possession structures.
The key for this back line is not just defending. It must support Spain’s high-possession game. If Spain want to dominate the ball in the opponent’s half, their defenders need to stand high, pass accurately, and react quickly after turnovers.

***

## 9\. Out of Possession: Spain Drop into 4\-1\-4\-1

Without the ball, Spain are likely to shift from 4-3-3 into a **4-1-4-1**.
Oyarzabal or Olmo stays up front.
Nico and Yamal drop into the wide midfield positions.
Pedri and Fabian Ruiz or Gavi form the central midfield line.
Rodri stays alone as the defensive midfielder.
The back four remain compact.

### ![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/06/20260609-115808-c1939d22-image.png)

The purpose of this defensive shape is central protection.
Spain are not usually a team that sits deep in their own box. Their more common approach is to keep good distances in midfield, block central passing lanes, and then press the ball when the timing is right.
Rodri’s position is crucial. He must stand between the defensive line and midfield line, stopping opponents from receiving freely between the lines. If Rodri is pulled out of position, Spain’s centre-backs can become exposed.
So Spain’s defending is not just about pressing hard. It is about spacing, timing, and positional discipline.

***

## 10\. Key Positional Roles

**Unai Simon: Sweeper Keeper**
Unai Simon must not only make saves, but also participate in build-up. His passing stability affects how smoothly Spain can progress from the back.
**Cubarsi: Ball-Playing Centre-Back**
Cubarsi is a key build-up defender. He needs to pass through pressure into Rodri, Pedri, or the full-backs.
**Laporte: Experienced Centre-Back**
Laporte provides experience, left-footed passing, and defensive stability.
**Cucurella: Aggressive Full-Back**
Cucurella supports Nico on the left, presses forward, and recovers quickly in defensive transitions.
**Porro: Attacking Full-Back**
Porro can provide width on the right and help Spain progress the ball in possession.
**Rodri: Anchor Man / Deep-Lying Playmaker**
Rodri is Spain’s midfield core. He controls tempo and protects Spain during transitions.
**Pedri: Left Half-Space Playmaker**
Pedri receives between midfield and attack, turning possession into real attacking threat.
**Fabian Ruiz / Gavi: Control or Pressing Option**
Fabian offers passing, control, and late runs. Gavi offers pressing, duels, and counter-pressing.
**Nico Williams: Wide Winger**
Nico gives Spain width, speed, and one-vs-one threat on the left.
**Lamine Yamal: Inverted Winger**
Yamal cuts inside from the right to create, shoot, or play the final pass.
**Oyarzabal / Olmo: Mobile Striker or False 9**
Oyarzabal gives Spain a clearer finishing point. Olmo gives Spain more central fluidity.

***

## 11\. Spain’s Biggest Strength: Possession Is No Longer Just Sideways Passing

The most valuable change in this Spain team is that they can still control possession, but they no longer keep the ball just for the sake of keeping it.
Rodri controls the tempo.
Pedri connects the half-spaces.
Nico and Yamal create one-vs-one threats out wide.
Oyarzabal or Olmo gives the central attack flexibility.
Cubarsi and Laporte allow Spain to build from the back.
This means Spain can pin opponents back through possession, but they can also attack directly through wide speed.
Older Spain looked more like a team trying to slowly pass through a wall.
This Spain first stretches the wall with possession, then attacks it with speed from wide areas.
That is what makes this team so interesting.

***

## 12\. Spain’s Weaknesses: Central Finishing and Space Behind the Full\-Backs

Spain still have possible weaknesses.
The first is central finishing.
If Oyarzabal starts, Spain have a clearer central target, but may lose some fluidity.
If Olmo starts centrally, Spain become more flexible, but may lack a natural penalty-box finisher.
The second issue is space behind the full-backs.
If Cucurella and Porro both push high, Spain can leave space behind them. In that case, Rodri and the two centre-backs must be excellent in defensive transition.
The third issue is the consistency of young players.
Yamal, Nico, and Cubarsi are all very talented, but World Cup knockout matches bring a different level of pressure. Spain will need them to stay calm and disciplined, not just rely on inspiration.

***

## 13\. Conclusion: A New Spain Built on Positional Play and Wide Explosiveness

Spain in 2026 are no longer just a traditional tiki-taka team.
They still have the possession DNA: Rodri, Pedri, Fabian, and Olmo can all help control the ball.
But they also have the directness modern football demands: Nico and Yamal can beat defenders wide, while Porro and Cucurella provide depth from full-back.
This Spain team are likely to use:
A 4-3-3 base formation.
A 3-2-5 attacking structure in possession.
A 4-1-4-1 defensive shape out of possession.
Rodri as the tempo controller.
Pedri as the midfield connector.
Nico and Yamal as wide one-vs-one threats.
Oyarzabal or Olmo to define the central attacking role.
The best phrase to describe this Spain team is:
**positional play with wide explosiveness.**
They have positional order, but they also have wide attacking power.
If Spain can solve the central finishing question and protect the space behind their full-backs, they could be one of the most tactically complete and entertaining teams at the 2026 World Cup.
