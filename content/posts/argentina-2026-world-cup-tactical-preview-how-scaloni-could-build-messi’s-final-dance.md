The 2026 World Cup is about to begin, and Argentina will enter the tournament as defending champions. According to currently available information, Argentina have been drawn into Group J, with Algeria, Austria, and Jordan as their group-stage opponents. Lionel Scaloni has also named a 55-man preliminary squad, including Lionel Messi, Lautaro Martínez, Julián Álvarez, Enzo Fernández, Alexis Mac Allister, Rodrigo De Paul, Cristian Romero, Lisandro Martínez, and Emiliano Martínez.
But the most interesting part of Argentina is not simply “what formation they use on paper.” The real question is how Scaloni allows the team to shift between different tactical shapes: in possession, Argentina may look like a 4-3-3 or even a 3-2-5; out of possession, they often drop into a compact 4-4-2 defensive block.

## **1\. Argentina’s Most Likely Base Formation: 4\-3\-3\, but Messi Makes It Fluid**

![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/05/20260523-003402-97cf5c90-image.png)
On paper, Argentina can easily be described as a 4-3-3:
Goalkeeper: Emiliano Martínez
Defenders: Molina / Romero / Lisandro Martínez / Tagliafico or Barco
Midfielders: De Paul / Enzo Fernández / Mac Allister
Forwards: Messi / Lautaro Martínez or Julián Álvarez / Nicolás González or Garnacho
But this is only the “kick-off screenshot.” In actual matches, Messi will not remain fixed as a traditional right winger. He often drifts inside, moves into central zones, or drops closer to midfield to receive the ball. As a result, Argentina’s 4-3-3 frequently turns into a 4-3-1-2 or a diamond-like 4-4-2.
That is what separates Argentina from many traditional 4-3-3 teams. They do not rely purely on wide wingers beating defenders one-on-one. Instead, they use Messi’s movement into central pockets to create numerical advantages, while players like Álvarez, Lautaro, Mac Allister, and De Paul rotate around him.
