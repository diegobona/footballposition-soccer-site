*What is the difference between a No. 6 and a No. 8?*

| **Quick Answer** |
| ------------ |
| A No. 6 usually plays deeper and focuses on protecting the back line, receiving from the centre-backs, and controlling build-up. A No. 8 plays higher and does more carrying, connecting, covering, and late arriving in attack. The simplest way to remember it: the No. 6 keeps the team stable; the No. 8 helps move it forward. |

**Where Do a No. 6 and a No. 8 Play?**
In a classic 4-3-3, the No. 6 is the deepest midfielder, sitting in front of the centre-backs. The No. 8s start higher, usually on either side of the No. 6. They are still central midfielders, but they operate further up the pitch and have more freedom to support attacks.

*Base positions in a 4-3-3: the No. 6 sits deeper, while the No. 8s start higher.*
**What Does a Number 6 Do?**
The first job of a No. 6 is to keep the middle of the pitch under control. This player gives the team a safe passing option in front of the defence and helps move the ball out from the back.

* Receive from the centre-backs
* Protect the space in front of the back line
* Control possession and set the passing rhythm
* Block central counters and cover for advanced full-backs
* Keep the team balanced when others push forward

A top No. 6 does not always make the most tackles. Often, the real value is positioning, timing, and knowing when to stay. Rodri and Sergio Busquets are classic examples.
**What Does a Number 8 Do?**
The No. 8 has a broader up-and-down role. This player helps in midfield battles, carries the ball forward, supports the attack, and often arrives late near the box.

* Carry the ball through midfield
* Link the deeper build-up with the attack
* Support wide areas and half-spaces
* Press, recover, and cover ground
* Make late runs into the box or edge of the box

Steven Gerrard, Federico Valverde, and Jude Bellingham are easy examples. They do not just sit in one lane; they affect several phases of the game.
**Number 6 vs Number 8: 5 Key Differences**

| **Area** | **No. 6** | **No. 8** |
| ---- | ----- | ----- |
| **Starting position** | Deeper, in front of the centre-backs | Higher, usually either side of the No. 6 |
| **Main task** | Protect, receive, control | Progress, connect, arrive |
| **Movement range** | More fixed | Larger and more dynamic |
| **Defending** | Positioning and screening | Covering, pressing, recovering |
| **Attacking contribution** | Build-up and circulation | Carrying, support, late runs, shots |

**1\. Positioning: The No\. 6 Starts Deeper**
This is the clearest difference. The No. 6 normally stays behind the ball. Even when the team is attacking, this player cannot vacate the central safety zone too easily because that space becomes vital if possession is lost.
**2\. The No\. 6 Thinks About Balance\, the No\. 8 Thinks About Progress**
When a No. 6 gets the ball, the first question is usually: how do we keep control? When a No. 8 gets the ball, the first question is often: how do we move the attack forward? That is why No. 6s play more stabilising passes, while No. 8s are more likely to carry or break lines.
**3\. Their Defending Looks Different**
The No. 6 defends mainly through positioning, screening passing lanes, and protecting the area in front of the centre-backs. The No. 8 does more running: closing down, recovering, helping wide, and covering space after turnovers.
**4\. The No\. 8 Gets Closer to the Box**
A classic No. 8 often makes late runs into the final third. Frank Lampard built a huge part of his game on this pattern, but many modern No. 8s do it too. The No. 6 usually stays deeper and chooses moments more carefully.
![](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/09/20260915-045927-302feb67-number-6-vs-number-8-in-soccer-en-integrated-image-2.png)
*Movement range: the No. 6 protects and controls; the No. 8 progresses, supports, and arrives.*
**5\. The No\. 6 Controls the Match\, the No\. 8 Changes Its Pace**
Elite No. 6s often decide where the ball goes next and when the team should slow down or speed up. A No. 8 is more likely to change the game with a forward carry, an extra run, or a sudden burst into space.

**Can a Player Be Both a No. 6 and a No. 8?**
Yes. Modern roles are not fixed. A player can look like a No. 6 in one system and a No. 8 in another. The best test is not the shirt number. Look at how deep the player starts, what space they protect, and whether their first job is control or progression.
**Bottom Line**
A No. 6 is the base of the midfield. A No. 8 is the connector and driver. The No. 6 focuses more on protection, receiving, and control. The No. 8 focuses more on progression, coverage, and late attacking support. If a player mostly holds the space in front of the defence, that is a No. 6. If a player keeps moving the game forward while still working back, that is a No. 8.
