| **A shadow striker is an attacking player who operates behind the main striker.** He does not spend the whole game dropping deep to organize play, and he does not stay pinned against the center-backs. Instead, he uses the space created by the No. 9 and makes late runs into the box to shoot or deliver the final pass. |

# **Where Does a Shadow Striker Play?**

A shadow striker usually starts behind the center-forward, in the space between the opposition midfield and defensive lines. The most common starting position is the central attacking midfield role in a 4-2-3-1, or the free role behind a lone striker in a 4-4-1-1.
He looks like a No. 10 on the formation graphic, but plays closer to goal. He also resembles a second striker, although his starting position is normally deeper. The real clue is not the dot on the tactics board; it is how often he arrives late in the penalty area.

*The shadow striker works behind the No. 9, between the lines, and is always ready to attack the box.*

# **What Does a Shadow Striker Do?**

The value of the role is not how often he touches the ball. It is how often he appears in a dangerous position.
· Finds unmarked pockets between the opposition holding midfielder and center-backs.
· Attacks the space behind the striker when the No. 9 drops off, drifts wide or occupies the center-backs.
· Makes late runs from the edge of the box or the half-spaces into scoring positions.
· Combines with the striker and wingers without controlling the attack like a traditional No. 10.
· Helps press from the front, although his main value is still his direct goal threat.

# **Shadow Striker vs False 9**

Both roles operate centrally and may receive away from the defensive line, but their movement usually goes in opposite directions. A False 9 starts on the front line and drops back. A shadow striker starts deeper and runs forward.

| **Comparison** | **Shadow Striker** | **False 9** |
| ---------- | -------------- | ------- |
| Starting position | Behind the striker | Center-forward position |
| Main movement | Forward into the box | Back toward midfield |
| Main job | Score and attack space | Create, link play and pull out center-backs |
| Effect on the defense | Uses space opened by the striker | Creates space by leaving the front line |
| Simple description | A late-arriving finisher | A playmaking striker who drops off |

The simplest difference: the False 9 leaves the front line to create space; the shadow striker runs into that space.

# **Shadow Striker vs Number 10**

A shadow striker may wear the No. 10 shirt and may begin in the attacking midfield position, but he is not the same as a traditional No. 10. The classic playmaker is judged mainly on control, creativity and the final pass. The shadow striker is judged more on movement, penetration and goals.

| **Comparison** | **Shadow Striker** | **Traditional Number 10** |
| ---------- | -------------- | --------------------- |
| Main responsibility | Make runs, shoot and create direct danger | Control attacks and play the final pass |
| Receiving position | Closer to the striker and penalty area | Between the lines or in deeper pockets |
| Off-ball movement | Frequently attacks the box | More often searches for passing angles |
| Goal expectation | Usually high | Depends on the player and system |
| Simple description | A scorer hidden behind the striker | The attacking conductor behind the striker |

Modern players rarely fit one label perfectly. Some can create like a No. 10 and still attack the box like a shadow striker. Their actual job matters more than the number on their shirt.

# **Best Shadow Striker Examples**

None of these players spent an entire career in one fixed role. But each of them showed the main features of a shadow striker in particular teams and systems.
![]() ![image](https://media.footballposition.soccer/uploads/2026/07/20260726-152833-d4871c6c-image.png)
**Thomas Müller:** Müller is neither a classic playmaker nor a fixed striker. His great strength is reading space that defenders cannot see. He waits between midfield and defense, then appears inside the box for the shot or assist.
**Dele Alli:** Under Mauricio Pochettino at Tottenham, Alli often played behind Harry Kane. When Kane dropped toward the ball or occupied the center-backs, Alli attacked from the second line. It was one of the clearest modern examples of the role.
**Antoine Griezmann:** Griezmann can play as a second striker or attacking midfielder. He drops to connect play, but his biggest threat comes when he finds space behind the center-forward and arrives in the box for the decisive action.
**Kaká:** At his peak, Kaká attacked from the No. 10 zone with speed, ball-carrying and direct forward runs. Compared with a more patient playmaker, his game was far more vertical and often looked like a shadow striker attacking from deep.

# **Which Formations Suit a Shadow Striker?**

· 4-2-3-1: the shadow striker starts as the central attacking midfielder behind the No. 9.
· 4-4-1-1: he becomes the second attacking threat behind a lone striker and receives plenty of freedom.
· 3-4-2-1: one of the two attacking midfielders can make repeated runs from the half-space.
· 4-3-1-2: the player behind the front two may attack second balls and make late runs into the box.
The role works best with a center-forward who can hold up the ball, occupy center-backs or drop toward midfield. Without that movement, the shadow striker may simply crowd the same spaces as the No. 9.

# **Is a Shadow Striker the Same as a Second Striker?**

They are closely related, but “second striker” is a broader position. “Shadow striker” describes a more specific style: a deeper starting point, fewer fixed duels with center-backs and frequent off-ball runs into the box. Many shadow strikers are second strikers, but not every second striker plays this way.

# **The Simple Explanation**

A shadow striker is the second goal threat behind the main striker.
He does not drop as often as a False 9, and he does not spend most of the game controlling possession like a traditional No. 10. His main job is to wait for the No. 9 to move the defense, then attack the gap from behind.
**Remember this:** the False 9 drops, the Number 10 creates, and the shadow striker runs forward.
